How to compare TikTok video hooks

Compare the opening, then choose one thing to test

A hook is the opening that gives someone a reason to keep watching. FrameNest puts public TikTok videos in one grid so you can review their openings without switching between tabs. Each player works separately; this is not synchronized playback or an automatic scoring tool.

1. Pick videos that answer the same question

For example, compare three product demonstrations rather than a product demonstration, a comedy sketch and a news clip. Similar goals make differences in the openings easier to describe. Use your own videos or material you have permission to use when publishing examples.

2. Describe what happens first

Paste the full public video links and create your grid. Play the beginning of each video and pause when you have enough to describe the opening. Note the first image, spoken line or caption. “Starts with the finished result” tells you more than “has a good hook.”

3. Separate observations from guesses

Here is a hypothetical comparison, not a test of real videos:

VideoOpening choiceWhat to consider
AShows the finished meal before the recipeThe result is visible before the steps begin.
BStarts with a question about cooking timeThe question introduces a specific problem.
CBegins by listing ingredientsThe information is useful, but the result is not yet visible.

This does not establish which video would perform best. Audience, topic, distribution and many other factors can affect results. The comparison helps you identify a choice you can test in your own edit.

4. Plan a small change

Write a concrete next step, such as “Show the finished meal before explaining the ingredients.” Keep the rest of your plan clear enough to understand what changed. Review the response to your own work rather than assuming someone else's opening will produce the same outcome.

Add your observations beside each video and download the notes before leaving. FrameNest does not score the videos for you.
